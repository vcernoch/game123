        How to play

        Click your blue unit, then a highlighted tile to move or an enemy with a yellow ring to attack. On a touch screen the first tap shows the path or a battle estimate and the second tap confirms it; the “Zoom map” button gives you bigger tiles. Enter a city to capture it. In your own city with no unit standing in it you can recruit, and you can build there at any time: walls (garrison +50 % defense; at the end of your turn the walls also shoot the weakest enemy next to the city for 2 HP – they never finish a unit off, and the AI's walls shoot back at you), a market (+2 gold), barracks (cheaper recruiting), a watchtower (the city sees 2 tiles farther), an infirmary (+3 healing, stops plague), a temple (+1 gold, no revolts) or an academy (recruits start as Veterans). You only see the surroundings of your units and cities (horsemen and units on hills see farther); explored land stays on the map. An enemy unit that disappears into the fog leaves a faded, dashed marker with “?” where you last saw it (or the tile it walked off into); hover over it to see how long ago. The marker fades away after 3 turns or as soon as you see that tile again. On roads (brown paths between cities) units move twice as fast. Crossing a river (blue lines on tile edges) costs an extra move point – not over a bridge – and a unit attacked in melee across a river defends 25 % better. At the start of your turn a bar below the header sums up what the enemy did to you (units lost or wounded, cities lost) and the tiles of your losses are marked with a red ✕ until you end the turn. Sometimes a random event hits you at the start of your turn: a bountiful harvest brings gold, plague takes HP from units in and around a city, and a revolt costs a city without a garrison its taxes. A unit that steps next to an enemy it can see must stop there (zone of control, marked with a small red stop sign) – it can still attack, and moving away from an enemy is free. Attacking an enemy that other units of yours stand next to is a flanking attack: +10 % attack for each of them, up to +30 % (the counterattack is not affected). A unit on hills attacking a unit below it has the high ground: +20 % attack. A horseman that has already moved at least 2 move points this turn charges: +25 % attack – but not against a unit in forest. Spearmen are strong against horsemen; catapults smash city garrisons and ignore walls; a cheap scout (Sc) moves 3, sees 4 tiles far, but can't enter or capture foreign cities. Units gain experience in battle and are promoted to Veteran (★) and Elite (★★) – each rank adds 15 % attack and defense. A unit that becomes a Veteran earns a name of its own (shown in gold in the unit panel and the army list) and keeps it for life; every unit counts its kills. A hero inspires the units of yours standing right next to it: +10 % defense (also against counterattacks; several heroes don't add up) – select a hero to see whom it inspires (gold ^ badge). The “Hall of fame” in the statistics lists your best units, including fallen ones – the enemy's heroes are revealed when the game ends. The “War timeline” below the charts lists the turning points you witnessed – first blood, captured cities, each side's first hero, famous heroes (2+ kills) that fell, who pulled ahead in strength and the victory – and small triangles under the strength chart mark them (hover for the story); the whole war is revealed when the game ends. A unit standing in your city that hasn't acted yet can be retrained in the side panel (warrior → spearman or horseman, archer → catapult, scout → horseman): it keeps its rank and share of HP, pays the price difference plus a small fee, and ends its turn. A unit you no longer need can be disbanded (✖ in the side panel or D, pressed twice): in your own city it returns 40 % of its price in gold, elsewhere nothing. Cities grow: every city you hold gains a size (the dots along the bottom of its tile, up to 3) after 5 of your turns, the next after 7 more, and each size above 1 adds +1 gold per turn. An enemy unit standing next to a city halts its growth while it stays there, and a captured city loses one size and starts growing anew – so a long siege hurts, and taking a big city back costs it. Neutral cities don't grow. Roads carry trade: a city of yours linked to your capital by an unbroken chain of road tiles (your own cities count as links, neutral and enemy cities don't) earns +1 gold per turn – the city panel shows its trade route. An enemy unit standing on the road cuts the route until it leaves, and a new road you build can link a city that had none. A city you take from the enemy is in unrest (a flame on its roof): it pays no gold and doesn't grow until it calms down at the start of your 4th turn after the capture – or already your 2nd with a garrison of yours inside, which calms it twice as fast (the AI leaves garrisons too). Neutral cities join you willingly. Every city of yours has a focus (the buttons in the city panel, or C to cycle): ⚖ Balanced has no bonus and no cost, 💰 Commerce gives +1 gold but the city stops growing, 🌾 Granary makes it grow twice as fast for −1 gold, and 🏯 Fortress gives its garrison +20 % defense for −1 gold (catapults ignore it, like walls). Income and growth are counted at the start of your turn, so the focus you leave at the end of a turn is the one that counts; a small badge next to the size dots shows it, and a captured city goes back to Balanced. The AI picks a focus for its cities too. In an emergency a city of size 2 or more can levy a free Warrior (the button under Recruit, or L): the city loses a size and starts growing anew, which costs it its extra gold; a restless city can't levy. The AI levies when an enemy is near and its treasury is empty. When a city can't recruit right now – not enough gold, or a unit stands in it – its recruit buttons turn into dashed ⏳ order buttons (the number keys work too): the ordered unit is bought by itself at the start of your next turn that has the gold and a free city tile, after income and random events (an hourglass on the city tile shows a waiting order, the banner tells you when it was bought). Press the same button again or “✖ Cancel order” to call it off; recruiting anything in that city fulfils the order, and a captured city loses it. You win when the AI loses all its cities. Every map has a number (shown in the side panel): type it into “Map number” in the new game dialog to play the same map again or share it, and after a game “↻ Same map again” restarts it with the same settings. Hover over a tile to see the movement path. Resources near cities count for the city whose territory they are in: a gold mine (gold nuggets) gives +2 gold per turn, horses (horseshoe) make horsemen recruited there 4 gold cheaper. A unit (not a scout) that hasn't attacked and still has a move left can pillage an enemy resource it stands on (🔥 Pillage or P): you get +6 gold, its turn ends, and the resource gives the enemy nothing for their next 4 turns (it burns on the map until it recovers). The AI raids your resources too, so guard your mines and pastures – a unit of yours that hasn't attacked and still has a move left can repair a pillaged resource in your territory it stands on (🔧 Repair or R) for 3 gold, and it works again right away (the AI repairs its own too). Ancient ruins (broken columns) lie scattered around the map, about as many near you as near the AI: the first unit of either side to stop on one explores it and finds treasure (+15 gold), a forgotten drill yard (+3 experience), an old map (the land around is revealed) or wandering warriors who join you. A ruin looted in the fog still shows until you see that tile again. Coloured borders mark city territory (up to 2 tiles around each city); a unit resting in your own territory heals +2 instead of +1. Click a known tile out of reach to give a move order: the unit marches there over several turns (dashed route with the number of turns) and stops when an enemy comes into sight. A city of yours can have a rally point (🚩 in the city panel or G, then click a tile): every unit recruited there gets a move order to the flag and sets off at the start of your next turn (if a unit of yours already stands on the flag, it heads for the nearest free tile next to it). A captured city loses its flag. Two neighbouring units of yours that both have a move left and haven't attacked can swap places (⇄ in the unit panel or S, then tap the unit with a blue ring): a fresh unit can relieve a wounded one at the front in a single step – the AI does this too. Undo works for a swap like for a move. A unit of yours that hasn't attacked and still has a move left can build a road on a tile of your territory (🛤 Build road or B) for 4 gold – its turn ends, and from then on moving between road tiles costs ½ for everyone, enemies included. The AI extends its own roads when it has gold to spare. 🧭 Explore (or X) sends a unit into the fog on its own: each turn it walks to where it would see the most unexplored land (detouring for known ruins), never enters foreign cities, and stops when an enemy comes into sight or nothing is left to explore. The ⚠ button (or T) shades the tiles that enemies you can see could attack next turn – darker red where several can reach; tiles next to known enemy cities with walls count too. The “💡 Advisor” panel suggests up to three things worth doing this turn, most urgent first – an unguarded city with an enemy nearby, a sure kill, a badly wounded unit within enemy reach, a city or ruin you can reach now, a pillaged resource to repair, a trade route cut by an enemy on the road, gold to spend on recruits or buildings; it only uses what you can see. When you recruit, it names a unit type that fits (★ in the city panel): spearmen against enemy horsemen nearby, a catapult for a close enemy city, archers behind a line of melee units, a scout while most of the map is unknown, a horseman to grab a neutral city. Click a hint to select the unit or city it is about; collapse the panel to hide it (remembered). The “Army” panel lists all your units – ready ones first, then marching, fortified, on watch and done – with HP and where they stand; click a row to select that unit. A unit that hasn't moved or attacked yet can make a forced march (🥾 in the unit panel or M): +1 move this turn for 2 HP, but it can't attack and won't heal at the end of the turn – handy to get a defender into a threatened city in time (it never drops below 1 HP; the AI does this too, and the advisor tells you when a forced march saves an unguarded city). Tab = next unit with moves left, Z = undo move (only for a move that revealed nothing new – no new tile or enemy), F = fortify (a unit that hasn't moved or attacked digs in: +25 % defense until it moves or attacks; Tab skips fortified units), W = sentry (👁 in the unit panel: the unit keeps watch where it stands and Tab and the end-turn reminder skip it, until an enemy comes into sight within 2 tiles at the start of your turn; a wounded unit rests until it is fully healed – the banner tells you who woke up and why; moving or giving it any order wakes it too), Enter = end turn (if some units can still act, the button shows how many and asks you to press again; Shift+Enter ends right away), Esc = cancel selection or skip animation (a click on the map works too), A or the ▶ button in the header = animation speed (normal, fast ⏩ or off ⏸; remembered).
